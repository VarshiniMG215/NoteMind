Normalization organizes tables to reduce redundancy. First normal form requires atomic values. Second normal form removes partial dependencies on a composite key. Third normal form removes transitive dependencies. ACID stands for atomicity, consistency, isolation and durability, the guarantees of a database transaction. An index speeds up reads using a structure such as a B-tree but slows down writes. A primary key uniquely identifies a row, while a foreign key links to the primary key of another table. An inner join returns only matching rows; a left join keeps all rows from the left table.
